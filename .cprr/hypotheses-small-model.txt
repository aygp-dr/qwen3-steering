================================================================================
CPRR HYPOTHESES: SMALL MODEL STEERING (Qwen3-0.6B)
What Makes Sub-1B Activation Steering Fundamentally Different
================================================================================

Generated: 2026-03-06
Model: Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B (28 layers, d_model=1024, 16q/8kv GQA, SwiGLU, 751M params)
Base model: Qwen/Qwen3-0.6B-Base (distilled from larger Qwen3 family)
Framework: CPRR (Conjecture -> Proof -> Refutation -> Refinement)

Context references:
  - setup.org: alpha > 0.35 degrades coherence; layers 14-20 best; cross-model
    transfer fails; multi-property steering causes coherence collapse
  - Elhage et al. 2022 (Toy Models of Superposition): lower d_model => denser
    superposition => more feature interference per direction
  - Razzhigaev et al. 2024 (Your Transformer is Secretly Linear): near-perfect
    linear inter-layer relationships (Procrustes ~0.99); smaller models may be
    MORE linear, reducing effective layer diversity
  - Queiroz Da Silva et al. 2025 (Steering off Course): steering methods show
    "substantial variability" across models; tested 1.5B-70B but NOT sub-1B
  - Van der Weij et al. 2024 (Extending Activation Steering): combining multiple
    steering vectors at same layer fails; separate layers per property works
  - Ainslie et al. 2023 (GQA): grouped KV heads compress representations that
    multi-head attention would keep separate
  - Qwen3 Technical Report (Yang et al. 2025): 0.6B variant is distilled, not
    trained from scratch at this scale

H-SM-1: SUPERPOSITION DENSITY THRESHOLD
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Conjecture:
  At d_model=1024, the effective rank of the residual stream at mid-layers
  (14-20) is below 200, causing any single steering vector to have non-trivial
  cosine similarity (>0.1) with at least 30% of the top-100 principal
  components of the activation distribution. This makes "clean" single-axis
  steering impossible -- every style vector inadvertently activates unrelated
  features, explaining the factual confounding noted in setup.org.

Rationale:
  Elhage et al. 2022 show that when d_model is small relative to the number
  of features the model must represent, features are packed into nearly-
  overlapping directions (superposition). Qwen3-0.6B must encode the same
  conceptual vocabulary as its larger parent (it was distilled, not trained
  on a reduced concept set), but into a 1024-dimensional space instead of
  2048+ (Qwen3-1.7B) or 3584 (Qwen3-4B). The ratio of concepts-to-dimensions
  is much worse. At 7B+ (d_model=4096), a random unit vector in the residual
  stream has expected cosine similarity ~1/sqrt(4096) ~ 0.016 with any given
  feature direction. At d_model=1024, this baseline is ~1/sqrt(1024) ~ 0.031,
  roughly 2x higher. But distillation compounds this: the student must
  preserve the teacher's feature set in fewer dimensions, likely increasing
  effective superposition density beyond what random packing would predict.

Test protocol:
  1. Run 500 diverse prompts through Qwen3-0.6B, capture residual stream
     activations at layers 10, 14, 17, 20 using register_forward_hook on
     model.model.layers[L].
  2. Compute PCA on the (500 * seq_len, 1024) activation matrix at each layer.
  3. Measure effective rank: number of principal components needed to explain
     90% of variance (use the numerical rank definition: sum(sigma_i) / max(sigma_i)).
  4. Compute the "terse" and "formal" steering vectors from actadd.py.
  5. Project each steering vector onto the top-100 PCs. Measure the fraction
     of PCs with |cos_sim| > 0.1.
  6. Repeat steps 1-5 for Qwen3-1.7B (d_model=2048) as a control.

Success criterion:
  - Effective rank at layer 17 of Qwen3-0.6B is < 200 (out of 1024).
  - The "terse" steering vector has |cos_sim| > 0.1 with >= 30% of top-100 PCs
    at 0.6B, versus < 15% at 1.7B.
  - The cosine similarity between the "terse" vector and the "formal" vector
    is > 0.15 at 0.6B (indicating feature collision), versus < 0.08 at 1.7B.

Expected outcome:
  CONFIRMED. The 0.6B model's residual stream is a low-rank bottleneck where
  steering vectors cannot avoid collateral activation of unrelated features.
  This is the root cause of "factual confounding" noted in setup.org and
  explains why multi-property steering collapses: the vectors are not
  independent in this compressed space.

Falsification:
  If effective rank > 400 and inter-vector cosine similarity < 0.05 at 0.6B,
  the superposition density hypothesis is wrong and the steering failures must
  have a different cause (e.g., insufficient model capacity for the steered
  task, not geometric interference).

H-SM-2: DISTILLATION CREATES ANISOTROPIC ACTIVATION GEOMETRY
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Conjecture:
  Qwen3-0.6B, because it was distilled from a larger teacher rather than
  pretrained from scratch at 0.6B scale, has a more anisotropic residual
  stream than a hypothetical 0.6B model trained from scratch would have.
  Specifically, the top-10 principal components of its residual stream
  activations explain > 60% of total variance (vs. an expected ~35-45% for
  a from-scratch model at this scale), and the dominant directions correspond
  to the teacher's most salient feature axes. This anisotropy means steering
  vectors must compete with extremely strong "distillation ruts" -- high-
  variance directions the model is locked into by the KD loss.

Rationale:
  Knowledge distillation via KL-divergence on logits forces the student to
  reproduce the teacher's output distribution. The student, having fewer
  parameters, achieves this by concentrating its representational capacity
  along the directions most important for matching the teacher's logit
  distribution. This creates a "distillation signature" in the activation
  geometry: a small number of directions carry most of the variance, and
  the model is highly sensitive to perturbations along those directions
  (because they directly affect the logit-matching loss surface). A steering
  vector that has any projection onto these dominant directions will be
  amplified or distorted unpredictably.

  The "Your Transformer is Secretly Linear" finding (Razzhigaev et al. 2024)
  suggests this effect is worse in smaller models: if inter-layer mappings
  are more linear, the anisotropy from distillation propagates unchanged
  through layers rather than being reshaped by nonlinear computation.

Test protocol:
  1. Capture residual stream activations at layers 5, 10, 14, 17, 22, 27
     for Qwen3-0.6B (distilled) on 1000 diverse prompts.
  2. Compute PCA. Measure variance explained by top-10 and top-50 PCs.
  3. Measure isotropy score: avg cosine similarity between a random sample of
     1000 activation vectors (following Ethayarajh 2019). Score near 0 = isotropic.
  4. As control, perform the same analysis on:
     (a) Qwen3-0.6B-Base (the pre-distillation base, if weights differ)
     (b) A from-scratch 0.6B model of comparable architecture (e.g., a
         similarly-sized OLMo or Pythia checkpoint) to isolate the
         distillation effect from the architecture effect.
  5. Compute the projection of the "terse" steering vector onto the top-10
     PCs. If > 40% of the vector's energy falls in these directions, it will
     be dominated by distillation ruts rather than style information.

Success criterion:
  - Top-10 PCs explain > 60% variance at layer 17 for distilled Qwen3-0.6B.
  - The same measurement on a from-scratch model of similar size yields < 45%.
  - Isotropy score for Qwen3-0.6B is > 0.4 (highly anisotropic), vs. < 0.25
    for the from-scratch control.
  - The steering vector's projection onto the top-10 PCs accounts for > 40%
    of its L2 norm.

Expected outcome:
  CONFIRMED. Distillation concentrates the activation geometry into a low-
  dimensional manifold that steering vectors cannot escape. This predicts that
  mean-centering the steering vector (Jorgensen et al. 2023) -- subtracting
  the mean activation -- should disproportionately help at 0.6B scale because
  it removes the dominant distillation-rut component. Testable corollary:
  mean-centered ActAdd should show a larger improvement delta at 0.6B than
  at 7B+ models.

Falsification:
  If the distilled and from-scratch models show comparable anisotropy (within
  10% relative difference on all metrics), then distillation is not creating
  a special geometric structure and the 0.6B steering difficulties are purely
  a function of d_model width, not training procedure.

H-SM-3: GQA KV-HEAD SHARING COMPRESSES STYLE INTO FEWER ATTENTION SUBSPACES
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Conjecture:
  In Qwen3-0.6B's GQA configuration (16 query heads, 8 KV heads), style/tone
  information is encoded in the shared KV representations and is therefore
  distributed across only 8 subspaces instead of 16. This means attention-
  level steering (injecting at individual attention head outputs) has half
  the degrees of freedom compared to standard MHA, and two query heads that
  share a KV head cannot be steered independently. Concretely: ablating or
  steering a single KV head will produce a larger and less predictable effect
  on generation style than ablating a single head in an MHA model of the
  same total d_model.

Rationale:
  In standard MHA, each head has independent K, V projections, so each head
  can independently learn to attend to different stylistic features (formality
  markers, sentence length patterns, hedging language, etc.). GQA forces
  pairs of query heads to share K and V, meaning style-relevant key-value
  associations are compressed. The Ainslie et al. 2023 GQA paper shows this
  works well for general quality but does not examine the implications for
  post-hoc intervention. At 0.6B with only 8 KV heads (head_dim = 1024/16 =
  64), each KV head is a 64-dimensional subspace serving two query heads.
  This is a tight information bottleneck for style.

  The practical implication: residual-stream steering (our current approach
  via actadd.py) may work better than attention-head-level steering at this
  scale, because the residual stream is the 1024-dim space BEFORE the GQA
  bottleneck splits it.

Test protocol:
  1. For layers 14-18, extract per-head attention patterns for 100 prompts
     in both "terse" and "verbose" styles using hooks on the attention module
     (model.model.layers[L].self_attn).
  2. For each KV head (0-7), compute the mean attention entropy difference
     between terse and verbose prompts. This measures how much style
     information each KV head carries.
  3. Perform single-KV-head ablation: zero out one KV head at a time during
     generation and measure (a) perplexity increase and (b) style classifier
     score change on a 50-prompt test set.
  4. Compare the per-head ablation effect size to a comparable MHA model.
     If no exact MHA equivalent exists, compare the variance across KV-head
     ablation effects (8 heads) to the variance across query-head effects
     (16 heads). Higher variance in KV ablation = more concentrated style info.
  5. Compare steering effectiveness:
     (a) Residual stream injection at layer L (current actadd.py approach)
     (b) Attention output injection: add steering vector only to the output
         of specific attention heads
     Measure style shift per unit of perturbation norm for both approaches.

Success criterion:
  - Ablating the most style-relevant KV head causes >= 2x the perplexity
    increase compared to ablating the most style-relevant individual query
    head.
  - The variance of style-classifier-score change across the 8 KV head
    ablations is >= 1.5x the variance across the 16 query head ablations,
    indicating style is unevenly concentrated in KV space.
  - Residual stream steering achieves higher style shift per unit perturbation
    norm than attention-head-level steering (ratio >= 1.3x).

Expected outcome:
  PARTIALLY CONFIRMED. GQA does compress style information into fewer
  subspaces, making per-head intervention coarser. However, the residual
  stream approach (which is what actadd.py uses) largely sidesteps this
  problem because it operates upstream of the GQA split. The practical
  prediction: attention-head-level steering methods (like ITI, Li et al. 2023)
  will underperform residual-stream methods disproportionately at 0.6B-GQA
  compared to their performance gap at 7B-MHA.

Falsification:
  If KV-head ablation and query-head ablation show comparable effect sizes,
  and attention-head steering works as well as residual-stream steering, then
  GQA's KV sharing does not meaningfully compress style information and is
  not a factor in small-model steering difficulty.

H-SM-4: THE NARROW-BUT-DEEP PARADOX -- 28 LAYERS ENABLE LAYER-DISTRIBUTED
         STEERING THAT COMPENSATES FOR LOW d_model
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Conjecture:
  Qwen3-0.6B's high depth-to-width ratio (28 layers / d_model=1024 = 0.027)
  compared to typical 7B models (32 layers / d_model=4096 = 0.008) creates a
  unique opportunity: distributing a steering signal across many layers at low
  per-layer alpha is MORE effective at 0.6B than at 7B, relative to single-
  layer injection. Specifically, injecting at 4+ layers simultaneously at
  alpha=0.05 each will outperform single-layer injection at alpha=0.20 in
  terms of the style-shift-to-coherence-loss ratio, and this advantage will
  be LARGER at 0.6B than at 7B.

Rationale:
  "Your Transformer is Secretly Linear" (Razzhigaev 2024) shows that
  consecutive transformer layers have near-linear mappings between them
  (Procrustes similarity ~0.99). In a narrow model, a single large
  perturbation at one layer gets amplified through the remaining layers
  (because there is less nonlinear "absorption" capacity per layer in a
  narrow residual stream). This causes the coherence collapse noted at
  alpha > 0.35.

  However, the same linearity means that SMALL perturbations at MANY layers
  accumulate approximately additively. If the perturbation at each layer is
  below the threshold where nonlinear dynamics amplify it chaotically, the
  cumulative effect is a controlled linear drift in activation space. With
  28 layers, there are many injection points available.

  Van der Weij et al. 2024 already showed that injecting individual vectors
  at different layers simultaneously is "promising" for multi-behavior
  steering. At 0.6B, this should be even more important because the per-layer
  perturbation budget is smaller (lower alpha threshold before collapse).

  The existing multilayer.py already implements this. The hypothesis predicts
  its superiority is MORE pronounced at 0.6B than literature reports for 7B+.

Test protocol:
  1. Define a style metric: train a simple logistic regression classifier on
     50 terse vs. 50 verbose completions (using sentence-transformers embeddings).
  2. Define a coherence metric: perplexity of the steered output under the
     UNSTEERED model (higher = more coherent disruption).
  3. Generate completions for 30 test prompts under these configurations:
     (a) Single-layer L=17, alpha=0.20
     (b) Single-layer L=17, alpha=0.10
     (c) Multi-layer L=[12,14,16,18], alpha=0.05 each
     (d) Multi-layer L=[10,12,14,16,18,20], alpha=0.033 each
     (e) Baseline (no steering, alpha=0)
  4. Compute style_shift = classifier_prob(terse) - baseline_prob(terse).
  5. Compute coherence_cost = perplexity_steered / perplexity_baseline.
  6. Compute efficiency = style_shift / coherence_cost for each configuration.
  7. If possible, repeat on a 7B model and compare the multi-vs-single ratio.

Success criterion:
  - Config (c) or (d) achieves efficiency >= 1.5x config (a) at 0.6B.
  - The same multi-layer configs at 7B show efficiency improvement of < 1.2x
    over single-layer at 7B, demonstrating that the advantage is scale-specific.
  - Config (d) with 6 layers at alpha=0.033 each achieves style_shift >= 80%
    of config (a) while keeping coherence_cost < 1.2 (vs. coherence_cost > 1.5
    for config (a)).

Expected outcome:
  CONFIRMED. The 28-layer depth of Qwen3-0.6B is its hidden advantage for
  steering. The model is narrow but deep enough that distributed low-alpha
  injection can "walk" the activation trajectory gradually toward the target
  style without triggering the catastrophic perturbation amplification that
  single-layer high-alpha injection causes. This predicts that the optimal
  steering strategy at 0.6B is qualitatively different from 7B: many layers,
  low alpha, rather than one layer, moderate alpha.

Falsification:
  If single-layer injection at alpha=0.20 outperforms or matches multi-layer
  injection in efficiency at 0.6B, the "narrow-but-deep" advantage does not
  exist and the 28-layer depth does not compensate for low d_model.

H-SM-5: CONTRASTIVE STEERING VECTORS HAVE A MINIMUM EFFECTIVE d_model
         AND 1024 IS NEAR THE BOUNDARY
------------------------------------------------------------------------

Conjecture:
  There exists a critical d_model threshold below which contrastive activation
  addition (ActAdd) fails to produce a vector that reliably separates the
  intended style axis from noise. At d_model=1024, Qwen3-0.6B is near this
  boundary: the signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of a contrastive steering vector
  (measured as the ratio of its projection onto the true style direction vs.
  its projection onto random directions) is between 2.0 and 4.0 -- enough
  for binary style shifts (terse/verbose) to sometimes work, but too low
  for fine-grained control (e.g., "slightly more formal" vs. "much more formal")
  or for multi-axis steering. At d_model >= 2048, the SNR exceeds 6.0, enabling
  graded and compositional steering.

Rationale:
  ActAdd computes vec = mean(act_pos) - mean(act_neg) from a SINGLE pair of
  prompts. The "signal" is the true style direction; the "noise" is everything
  else that differs between the two prompts (different token counts, different
  surface forms, attention pattern differences, etc.). In a 1024-dim space,
  two random unit vectors have expected |cos_sim| ~ 0.031. With only one
  contrastive pair, the prompt-specific noise is NOT random -- it is structured
  and may have magnitude comparable to the style signal.

  The corpus mean approach (corpus_style_vec.py) averages over N examples,
  which reduces noise by ~1/sqrt(N). But even with N=10 examples (as used
  in the project), the noise floor in a 1024-dim space may still be
  significant. The key prediction: at d_model=1024, increasing N from 2 to 10
  to 30 will show diminishing but still meaningful SNR gains, while at
  d_model=4096, even N=2 (pure ActAdd) already achieves sufficient SNR.

  This connects to the "Steering off Course" findings (Queiroz Da Silva 2025):
  steering methods show high variability, but their study only went down to
  1.5B. Below 1B, the variability should be even worse, and the failure rate
  should be predictably related to d_model.

Test protocol:
  1. Compute the "terse" steering vector via ActAdd (N=1 pair) at layer 17.
  2. Compute the "terse" vector via corpus mean with N=5, 10, 20, 30 examples.
  3. For each vector, measure:
     (a) Style discriminability: mean cos_sim of the vector with activations
         from 50 held-out terse examples, minus mean cos_sim with 50 verbose
         examples. This is the "style signal."
     (b) Noise floor: std of cos_sim across examples within each class.
     (c) SNR = style_signal / noise_floor.
  4. Apply each vector at alpha=0.15 and measure:
     (a) Binary style classification accuracy (terse vs. not-terse) on output.
     (b) Graded style measurement: cosine similarity between output embeddings
         and the "ideal terse" centroid, measured at alpha=0.05, 0.10, 0.15,
         0.20, 0.25. Does style increase monotonically with alpha? (Monotonicity
         = controllability.)
  5. Repeat steps 1-4 on Qwen3-1.7B (d_model=2048).

Success criterion:
  - At d_model=1024 with ActAdd (N=1 pair): SNR < 4.0.
  - At d_model=1024 with corpus mean N=10: SNR between 4.0 and 6.0.
  - At d_model=2048 with ActAdd (N=1 pair): SNR > 6.0.
  - Graded controllability (monotonic style increase with alpha) holds for
    only 60-70% of test prompts at d_model=1024, vs. 85%+ at d_model=2048.
  - At d_model=1024, corpus mean with N=30 approaches but does not reach the
    SNR that ActAdd achieves at d_model=2048 with N=1.

Expected outcome:
  CONFIRMED. d_model=1024 is near a practical boundary for contrastive
  steering. ActAdd barely works for binary axes; corpus mean is necessary
  but insufficient for graded control. This explains the project's observation
  that "binary style axes (terse/verbose, formal/casual)" work but "not
  nuanced blends." The implication: at sub-1B scale, contrastive steering
  should be treated as a binary switch, not a continuous dial. For graded
  control at this scale, one needs either (a) much larger N in corpus mean,
  (b) SAE-based feature identification to find a cleaner direction, or
  (c) multi-layer distributed injection (connecting to H-SM-4).

Falsification:
  If ActAdd (N=1) at d_model=1024 achieves SNR > 6.0 and graded monotonic
  controllability on > 85% of prompts, then d_model=1024 is NOT near the
  boundary and the steering difficulties at 0.6B have a different explanation
  (e.g., model capacity, training data, or architectural factors other than
  width).

CROSS-HYPOTHESIS DEPENDENCIES AND EXPERIMENTAL ORDERING
================================================================================

Recommended execution order:

  1. H-SM-1 (Superposition density)
     -- Foundational: PCA analysis needed by H-SM-2 and H-SM-5
     -- Cheapest experiment (just forward passes + PCA, no generation)

  2. H-SM-2 (Distillation anisotropy)
     -- Reuses PCA data from H-SM-1
     -- Requires a from-scratch control model (Pythia-410M or OLMo-1B)

  3. H-SM-5 (Minimum effective d_model)
     -- Reuses steering vectors from H-SM-1
     -- Requires Qwen3-1.7B as control (additional compute)

  4. H-SM-3 (GQA compression)
     -- Requires attention-level hooks (more complex instrumentation)
     -- Independent of H-SM-1/2 results

  5. H-SM-4 (Narrow-but-deep distributed steering)
     -- Requires generation + evaluation pipeline (most expensive)
     -- Results of H-SM-1 inform alpha choices

Key predictions from the ensemble:
  - If H-SM-1 + H-SM-2 + H-SM-5 all confirm, the prescription for 0.6B
    steering is: use corpus-mean vectors (not single-pair ActAdd), mean-center
    them, distribute across 4-6 layers at low alpha, and treat style control
    as binary rather than graded.
  - If H-SM-3 confirms, add: avoid attention-head-level intervention methods
    (ITI etc.) at this scale; residual-stream injection is strictly preferred.
  - If H-SM-4 confirms, the 28-layer depth is an asset, not neutral. The
    optimal configuration at 0.6B is qualitatively distinct from 7B recipes.
